## About Me

I'm Sina Rismanchian, a PhD candidate in Education at UC Irvine, working with [Shayan Doroudi](https://sites.google.com/uci.edu/shayan-doroudi). My research draws on constructionism to study the [interactions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-025-00483-9) of artificial intelligence and education, where I use AI as an object-to-think-with about educational research and study students' and teachers' interactions with AI as an object-to-think-with. My recent research focuses on undergraduate students' usage of AI as an object to **not** think with in academic settings.

Prior to UC Irvine, I completed my Bachelor's degree in Computer Engineering at Sharif University of Technology in Iran. I was born and raised in Isfahan, one of the most ancient cities in Iran. 

## Research Interests

My research interests span several key areas:

- **Deep Learner-GenAI Interactions**: I am fascinated by the idea of students and teachers using AI to create enhance their own learning, create personally meaningful artifacts, or create something they have not been able to otherwise. I study and design these cases of deeper interactions between learners and GenAI. Researching ways to promote deeper interactions such that learners "program the AI, rather than AI program the learner". These deeper interactions can look like a child using AI to [reflect on her own thinking](https://osf.io/preprints/edarxiv/64ekv_v1), a teacher creating an AI chatbot to empower their students and peers, and so on. 
- **Shallow Learner-GenAI Interactions**: I also study non-examples of deeper interactions, where students use AI to complete their academic tasks without genuinely engaging with learning opportunities (aka cheating.) I aim to mitigate shallow interactions by advancing students' [AI literacy](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED671128), knowledge of intellectual virtues, and co-desiging learning experiences that showcase the potentials for deeper interactions.
- **AI as Computational Models of Learning**: Developing computational models of learning to transform abstract theories to interrogable computaitonal objects such as my work on [ABICAP](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-36272-9_73) (on ICAP theory) and [Procedural ABICAP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13364) (to reconcile Congnitive Load Theory, KLI, and ICAP). Furthremore, I research  language models as models of students' mathematical misconceptions; more on that later...
